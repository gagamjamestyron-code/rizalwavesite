A life in context · 1861–1896

More than a name in a history book.

José Rizal was a physician and writer who lived under Spanish colonial rule. Through his novels and essays, he examined the conditions around him and contributed to wider discussions about reform, justice, and education in the Philippines.

Early life and education

José Protacio Rizal Mercado y Alonso Realonda was born on June 19, 1861, in Calamba, Laguna. He grew up in a family that valued education and developed a strong interest in reading and learning.

Rizal studied in the Philippines and later in Europe. He pursued medicine and became an ophthalmologist, a doctor who specializes in eye care. His studies and travels introduced him to new ideas and influenced how he wrote about society.

Life, reform, and public thought

Rizal used his knowledge and writing to reflect on the social conditions of his time. He questioned unequal treatment and criticized injustices he saw around him. His life and work became significant parts of Philippine history.

Dapitan and his final years

In 1892, Rizal was exiled to Dapitan in Mindanao. During his time there, he continued his work and served the local community. In 1896, he was executed in Manila. His death became a major event in Philippine history.
